The DPDP Act 2023: What It Means for Indian Startups

Sneha Gupta• January 10, 2026• 5 min read

A New Era of Data Privacy

The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023, has fundamentally changed how Indian businesses must handle user data. The days of unchecked data collection are over. For startups, compliance is no longer optional—it's a survival requirement.

Key Concept: You are likely a 'Data Fiduciary'

If you determine the purpose and means of processing data, you are a Data Fiduciary. This comes with specific responsibilities:

  • Notice & Consent: You must provide a clear itemised notice before collecting data, and obtain verifiable consent. No more pre-ticked boxes.
  • Purpose Limitation: You can only use the data for the specific purpose the user agreed to.
  • Data Principal Rights: Users have the right to access summary of their data, correct it, or demand erasure.

The Cost of Non-Compliance

The Act introduces significant penalties for breaches—up to ₹250 Crore. Unlike previous laws, there are no criminal penalties (jail time), but the financial hit can be fatal for a small business.

Immediate Steps for Startups

  1. Data Audit: Map out what data you collect and why. Delete what you don't need.
  2. Update Privacy Policies: Ensure they are simple, available in local languages (where applicable), and explicitly state purpose.
  3. Consent Manager: Implement a robust Consent Management Platform (CMP) to track approvals.

Conclusion

The DPDP Act brings Indian standards closer to GDPR. While it adds friction initially, it ultimately builds high trust with consumers. Start your compliance journey today to avoid future regulatory hurdles.
